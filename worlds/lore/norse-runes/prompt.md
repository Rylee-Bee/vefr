# Norse runes - copy-paste prompt

Use this prompt with any LLM (Claude, ChatGPT, your local
llama.cpp, whatever you prefer) to do the *real* research pass
before building a rune-cast-flavored world. The engine's
`/api/lore/preview` endpoint wraps this in a one-shot call, but
you can also paste it manually.

---

You're a research assistant helping me build a playable world
where the runes are the way the town knows what it's doing.
The runes are Norse (Elder Futhark, 24 runes in three aettir),
and they're not fortune-telling - they're **seeing the situation
honestly**. The runes tell you what the world is doing *now*,
in this place, in this moment.

This isn't about rune magic. It's about a town where the way
to know what's happening is to *stop and read*.

Here's what I'm working from:

[PASTE YOUR SEED WORDS, MOOD, OR EXISTING IDEAS HERE]

[example: "a bell at the water, kept by a town that doesn't
know what it rings for anymore"]
[example: "the protagonist is named wrong by the town, and the
runes are how the town names her"]
[example: "I want the Hero's Journey told in rune-verse, where
each phase is a rune"]

## What I want back

### 1. Three textures (2-4 paragraphs each)
A "texture" is the feel of a thing - what it sounds like, smells
like, who walks through it, what they do when no one's looking.

Pick three of these and write one paragraph each:

- a **place** the player will walk through (the threshold, the
  well, the hearth, the room where the chips are kept)
- a **person** the player will talk to (the reader of the runes,
  the one who carved the threshold, the one who keeps the bag)
- a **moment** the world keeps asking the player to come back to
  (the cast, the bell, the crossing)

Don't try to be complete. Try to be *felt*. The point isn't to
build the world - it's to give me words that make me want to.

### 2. A handful of rune names (5-10)
Pick from the 24 Elder Futhark runes - or, if your world needs
new runes, name them in the same shape (a single-syllable
Old Norse word that names a thing).

For each rune, give:

- the **rune name**
- one sentence on **what it sounds like** when said
- one sentence on **what the rune belongs to** in your world

### 3. The questions I'd ask you next
Write 2-4 questions - the ones that make me realize the world has
depths I haven't seen yet.

## What I don't want

- A history lesson about runes.
- A list of rune meanings.
- Anything that pretends the runes predict the future.
- Anything that sounds like "now you can do rune magic."

## Tone rules

- **Show, don't explain.** A texture is *what you see when you
  walk in*, not *what the runes are for*.
- **The runes tell you what's happening *now*.** To the past is the
  witness. To the future is the road. The runes are *this
  moment*.
- **The reader is not the speaker.** The chips say the rune;
  the reader holds the chips. The reader's voice is a vessel.
- **Roadside prose, not library prose.** Brevity over completeness.
  A little crooked over polished.

## Sources

The Elder Futhark rune poems are in the **public domain** in every
jurisdiction (they are between 800 and 1100 years old). The
translations used in this pack are from the Anglo-Saxon and Old
Norwegian rune poems, both public domain since at least the
1960s.

The most useful modern source: **Stephen Flowers, *The Northern
Runes* (1984)** - quoted under fair use for reference only.
Stephen Flowers' work is itself a derivative of the public-domain
rune poems; our lore pack cites only the original poems.

For the *texture*, you don't need sources. You need **listening**.
Read rune poems. Sit with what they say. Cast some chips.
