# Norse runes - the word-hoard

Twenty-four runes, in three aettir (groups of eight). Each rune
has a name, a stave (shape), and a rune poem. The poem is the
canonical reading. The interpretations in `cards.md` are the
engine's own - they tie each rune to a phase of the journey.

## First aettir (the gods / the upper)
1. **Fehu** (f) - wealth, cattle, the seed-fire
2. **Uruz** (u) - the aurochs, the wild ox, the slow strength
3. **Thurisaz** (th) - the thorn, the giant, the gateway
4. **Ansuz** (a) - the god (Odin), the word, the message
5. **Raido** (r) - the ride, the road, the going
6. **Kenaz** (k) - the torch, the ulcer, the known
7. **Gebo** (g) - the gift, the exchange, the balance
8. **Wunjo** (w/v) - joy, the wind-smooth stone, the comfort

## Second aettir (the natural world / the middle)
9. **Hagalaz** (h) - hail, the seed-grain, the breaking
10. **Nauthiz** (n) - need, the constraint, the trouble
11. **Isa** (i) - ice, the stillness, the holding
12. **Jera** (j/y) - the year, the harvest, the return
13. **Eihwaz** (ei) - the yew, the axis, the endurance
14. **Perthro** (p) - the dice-cup, the hidden, the lot-cast
15. **Algiz** (z) - the elk-sedge, the protection, the sanctuary
16. **Sowilo** (s) - the sun, the victory, the whirling

## Third aettir (the deep / the lower)
17. **Tiwaz** (t) - Tyr, the star, the right hand
18. **Berkano** (b) - the birch, the birth, the bearer
19. **Ehwaz** (e) - the horse, the partnership, the yoke
20. **Mannaz** (m) - the human, the self, the ash-tree
21. **Laguz** (l) - the water, the leek, the going-by-float
22. **Ingwaz** (ng) - the seed, the gestation, the inward
23. **Othala** (o) - the heritage, the homeland, the inheritance
24. **Dagaz** (d) - the day, the dawn, the breakthrough

---

The rune poems that follow below are drawn from the Anglo-Saxon
and Old Norwegian rune poems (8th-12th century, public domain).
The poems are short - typically two lines each. They are the
canonical readings. Interpretations in `cards.md` are the
engine's own additions.

## Rune poems (Anglo-Saxon, public domain)

> **Fehu** - Wealth is a comfort to all men;
> yet must every man bestow it freely, if he wish to gain honour
> in the sight of the Lord.

> **Uruz** - The aurochs is fierce and gory,
> horned and hide-bound; a mighty fighter.

> **Thurisaz** - The thorn is sharp and severe,
> an evil thing for any knight to touch;
> uncommonly severe upon all those who sit among the brambles.

> **Ansuz** - The mouth is the source of all language,
> a support of wisdom and a comfort of sages;
> a blessing and a joy to those who know it well.

> **Raido** - Riding is easy for the warrior
> who sits in the stirrups, far from home;
> but harsh is the journey for the man who walks alone.

> **Kenaz** - The torch is known by its burning,
> a flame most often kindled by the living;
> the wanderer's guide and the homesitter's friend.

> **Gebo** - Generosity brings credit and honour,
> which support one's dignity;
> it furnishes help and shelter
> to those who are broken and need it.

> **Wunjo** - Joy is a comfort to those who know it,
> hope to the fearful and a harbour to the homefast;
> a a light to the weary and a staff to the stumbling.

> **Hagalaz** - Hail is the coldest of grains;
> it whirls down from the heavens
> and is the most wrathful of weather.

> **Nauthiz** - Trouble is oppressive to the heart;
> yet often it becomes a help to the sons of men,
> if they heed it aright in time.

> **Isa** - Ice is the bark of rivers,
> and the roof of houses,
> and a danger to the unwary.

> **Jera** - The harvest is a joy to men,
> when the gods let the earth bring forth
> her fruits for the rich and the poor alike.

> **Eihwaz** - The yew is an unfriendly tree,
> hard to bend and brittle by nature,
> a guardian of flame, a nourisher of joy.

> **Perthro** - The dice-cup is the luck of the play,
> the waster of wealth and the gladdener of friends;
> it is the comfort of the lonely and the bane of the miser.

> **Algiz** - The elk-sedge grows in the marsh,
> a wound-stancher and a blood-stancher,
> a stay against all who would work harm.

> **Sowilo** - The sun is ever the traveller's friend,
> the sailor's joy, the giver of grain,
> the brightener of cities and the warmer of hearts.

> **Tiwaz** - Tyr is a star, the keeper of the sky,
> the brightener of the heavens and the hope of men;
> the steadfast friend of the warrior.

> **Berkano** - The birch is the leafiest of trees,
> with branches that bear the burden of fruit;
> yet the slenderest of timber, the kindest of woods.

> **Ehwaz** - The horse is the joy of the warrior,
> the wealth of the wanderer, the partner of the lord;
> the comfort of the rider and the carrier of the dead.

> **Mannaz** - The human is the delight of the home,
> the comfort of the kin, the warmth of the fire;
> yet often the wanderer, often the weary.

> **Laguz** - Water is the river's blood,
> the lake's mirror, the sea's depth;
> a danger to the unwary and a friend to the swimmer.

> **Ingwaz** - The seed is the promise of the field,
> the gestation of the sown, the inheritance of the harvest;
> the patience of the soil and the wonder of the growing.

> **Othala** - The heritage is the home of the home,
> the inheritance of the lineage and the law of the kindred;
> a shelter for the living and a song for the dead.

> **Dagaz** - The day is the dawn's own,
> the brightener of mornings and the promise of noons;
> the hope of the hopeful and the comfort of the waiting.
