# Norse runes - texture

The runes are the Norse version of tarot. Not fortune-telling -
**seeing the situation honestly**. The cast is the act of throwing
the chips and reading the positions. The reading is the act of
saying what the chips say, without adding to it.

The runes work because they are *few*. Twenty-four runes, eight
in each of three groups (the three *aettir* of the Elder Futhark).
The reader doesn't pick the rune; the cast does. The reader doesn't
interpret the rune; the rune poem does. What the reader does is
*say it*.

When you build a rune-cast-flavored world, the player is walking
through a place where the way to know what's happening is to *stop
and read*. The bell, the rumors, the kept items - each of these is
a rune. Each one is what the cast has thrown. The player reads the
cast; the cast doesn't read the player.

A town in this flavor feels like:

- **There are more runes than words.** The town speaks fewer
  words than the runes do. The bell rings once - *Laguz*, water.
  The stranger stands at the well - *Wunjo*, joy. The keeper has
  a name they don't say out loud. The runes are the parts of the
  language the town can hold.

- **The cast is the moment.** When the chips land, the reading is
  *now*. Not the future. The runes tell you what the world is
  doing this minute, in this place. To read the future you would
  have to read the past - but the runes don't do that either.

- **The reader is not the speaker.** The reader holds the chips;
  the chips say the rune. The reader's voice is just a vessel.
  The runes speak through the person, and the person is changed
  by having spoken them.

- **The cast is small.** Not a great wheel of fortune. A handful
  of chips on a cloth. Whatever the chips say, that is the cast.
  Whatever isn't there is not the cast's concern.

The model should produce textures that feel like *someone carved
this on a stick and handed it to you*. Not library prose. Carved
prose. Short, named, marked.
