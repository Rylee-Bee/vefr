## Sources this pack draws on

This pack is a derivative work. Its rune poems come from texts
that are themselves in the **public domain** in every jurisdiction
(they are between 800 and 1100 years old):

- **The Anglo-Saxon Rune Poem** - anonymous, ~8th-11th century
- **The Old Norwegian Rune Poem** - anonymous, ~12th century
- **The Icelandic Rune Poem** - anonymous, ~15th century

The rune poems quoted in `names.md` are the public-domain English
translations by George Stephens (*The Old-Northern Runic
Monuments of Scandinavia and England*, 1866-67) and Bruce Dickins
(*Runic and Heroic Poems of the Old Teutonic Peoples*, 1915),
both now in the public domain.

The 24 Elder Futhark rune names (Fehu, Uruz, Thurisaz, Ansuz,
Raido, Kenaz, Gebo, Wunjo, Hagalaz, Nauthiz, Isa, Jera, Eihwaz,
Perthro, Algiz, Sowilo, Tiwaz, Berkano, Ehwaz, Mannaz, Laguz,
Ingwaz, Othala, Dagaz) are the canonical Elder Futhark names.
They are in the public domain. The lore pack's *use* of them -
binding each to a phase of the Hero's Journey, weaving them with
the engine's phase structure - is the pack's own original creative
work, released under CC BY-SA 4.0 above.

## What this pack is NOT

This pack does **not** contain:
- A claim that runes have supernatural predictive power.
- Real-world occult instructions.
- Religious or content from any specific Norse-*pagan*
  denomination, tradition, or practice.
- Any historical or archaeological claim about how runes "were
  actually used" in any specific pre-modern Scandinavian community.

The runes are treated as **literature** - a poetic vocabulary
inherited from a tradition, not a magical system. The world
built from this lore pack is fiction. The lore pack is
*a literary mood-board for fiction*.

If you want to engage with the runes as a religious, magical,
or historical practice, please do so with sources appropriate
to that engagement (academic works, primary-source editions,
community resources, etc.). This lore pack is *not* a substitute
for those sources.
