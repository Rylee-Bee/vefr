# Historical event - texture

The historical-event flavor is **what the record couldn't hold**.

Real things happen to real people. The record takes the parts that
fit on paper - the names, the dates, the official acts - and leaves
out the parts that don't: the silence after the survivor stops
talking, the thing the witness saw that they didn't write down,
the name the family used at home that no archive kept.

When you build a historical-event-flavored world, the player is
walking through a place that **remembers wrong** - not from
malignance, but because memory is what survives the cut. The
record is right. The world is what the record left out.

A town in this flavor feels like:

- **The town knows what happened, but not how.** The bell rings.
  The bell has always rung. No one alive is sure when it stopped
  ringing for what it originally rang for, or what it rang for.
  The town rings it because ringing it is what the town does.

- **Witnesses are not heroes.** They are people who saw something
  and then lived with having seen it. The town may name a street
  after them, or may not. The witness does not need the street.
  The witness needs to be listened to once.

- **Names are taken and renamed.** The town's original name was
  something else. The street's name is the colonizer's name. The
  family's name is what they hid. The protagonist's name is the
  one she had to claim.

- **There is no "after".** The event didn't end. The bell is
  still ringing for it. The fire is still being passed. The
  wound is closed; the scar is not. The world is *how the town
  lives with what it remembers*, which is not "after."

The model should produce textures that feel like *someone said this
in a kitchen three weeks after*. Not academic. Not dramatic. Just
true, and a little worn from saying.
