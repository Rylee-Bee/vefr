# Historical event - word-hoard (suggested names)

Names in this flavor carry the weight of real people. They are
often:
- **the public name** (what the record used)
- **the home name** (what the family used; rarely in any archive)
- **the place name** (the street, the building, the road - named
  after someone, or renamed to forget someone)

A handful to seed the world's word-hoard. The author picks,
changes, or adds.

## The named kinds of witness
- **the bearer** - the one who carried something out
- **the looker-on** - the one who saw and didn't act, and lives
  with that
- **the one who came back** - returned, but not to the same place
- **the one who didn't come back** - and whose absence is the
  town
- **the recorder** - kept the names; not always a writer
- **the witness who broke** - what they saw broke them, and the
  breaking is part of the record

## Places
- **the hall of names** - where the record lives (a parish, an
  archive, a bookshop, a town hall)
- **the bell at the water** - whatever was supposed to call people
  home, but didn't
- **the road that goes inland** - the path the survivors walked
- **the street that was renamed** - the memory the town chose
  to bury
- **the unmarked corner** - the place where someone was laid to
  rest without ceremony, or with ceremony that the record didn't
  keep
- **the room that stays shut** - in someone's house; the room the
  protagonist isn't allowed into, or doesn't want to enter

## Things & attributes
- **the quiet that follows** - the silence after the bell stops
- **the load that was carried** - and the hands that carried it
- **the door that opened late** - the help that came after
- **the fire that was kept** - what the survivors kept burning
- **the name that was renamed** - the wound the town disguised
- **the day the bell didn't ring** - the day the ritual failed

## Epithets for the protagonist
- **the one who came back** - returned, with knowledge
- **the one who carries the name** - holds what the record couldn't
- **the one who reads the room** - sees what's not in the books
- **the one who asks the kitchen questions** - gets the truth
  from the people who weren't in the record

The list is a seed, not a catalog. Add what the world asks for.
