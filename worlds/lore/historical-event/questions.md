# Historical event - wandering questions

The questions a historical-event-flavored world asks. They are not
the questions a researcher asks. They are the questions a person
who is *inside* the event asks - sometimes out loud, more often
to themselves.

## About the event
1. **What did the bell ring for that day?**
   Every bell has a first time. Whatever it rang for that day is
   the bell's deepest memory. The town may have forgotten. The
   bell has not.

2. **Who went down to the water, and who stayed?**
   The event usually splits the town into the ones who left and
   the ones who couldn't. The protagonist is usually one of the
   second. So is everyone who tells her about the first.

3. **What name did the family use at home?**
   The official name is in the record. The home name was for the
   people who loved them. The protagonist will need to find it.

## About the people
1. **What did the witness see that they didn't write down?**
   The witness saw everything. They wrote down what they could
   hold on paper. What they couldn't hold is the world's deepest
   truth.

2. **What does the protagonist's family say about the event, and
   what do they not say?**
   The not-saying is the family record. It is kept warm. It is not
   offered. The protagonist will have to ask, and they will have
   to be ready to receive the answer.

3. **Who is the one person in town who knows the part the record
   doesn't have?**
   They are usually old. They are usually quiet. They are usually
   not a professional. They are the one who was there.

## About the place
1. **What does the bell ring for *now*?**
   Whatever the bell rang for then, it doesn't ring for that
   anymore. Whatever it rings for now is the town's new answer to
   the old question.

2. **Where is the room that stays shut?**
   In someone's house. The protagonist isn't supposed to know
   about it. The protagonist will know.

3. **What is the smallest thing the town has stopped doing?**
   Not the biggest forgetting. The smallest. The one no one
   notices. The protagonist will notice.

## About the ending
1. **Who gets to put the name on the record?**
   Whoever does, it stands. The protagonist's choice is whether
   to ask them to.

2. **What does the bell toll for, the last time?**
   The first tolling is the question. The last is the answer.
   In this flavor, the answer may be that the bell can be
   taken down now, or that it can't.
