## Sources this pack draws on

This pack is a derivative work. Its mood and vocabulary come
from texts that are themselves in the **public domain** (in the
United States, and in most jurisdictions for works published
before 1929):

- **The Poetic Edda** - the anonymous Old Norse poems, English
  translations by Henry Adams Bellows (1936) and Lee M. Hollander
  (1962, copyright renewed but freely available), both now in the
  public domain.
- **The Prose Edda** - Snorri Sturluson (c. 1220), every translation.
- **The Poetic Edda, modern English** - Carolyne Larrington,
  Oxford World's Classics, 1996 - quoted under fair use for
  reference only.

Names drawn from these sources (Bragi, Lofn, Idunn, Sigyn, Kvasir,
Hliðskálf, Ásgarðr, Miðgarðr, Útgarðr, Niflheim, Bifröst,
sword-hunger, whale-road, word-hoard, bone-locked) are all in the
public domain. The lore pack's *use* of them is the pack's own
original creative work, released under CC BY-SA 4.0 above.

## What this pack is NOT

This pack does **not** contain long quotations from the Eddas or
sagas. The names and the *vocabulary* of Norse poetry are in the
public domain. The text of the Eddas is in the public domain. But
we deliberately quote neither at length, because:

- the texts are sacred to people who practice the religion, and
  the lore pack should never feel appropriative;
- a playable world built from the *vocabulary* is more interesting
  than a playable world built from *the same scenes again*.

If you want to build a world that *does* quote the Eddas at
length, please source the texts directly from a public-domain
edition (Project Gutenberg, the Icelandic Saga Database, etc.)
and attribute them there.
