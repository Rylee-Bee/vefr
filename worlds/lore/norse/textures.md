# Norse - wandering poets texture

The Norse flavor is not the Eddas-as-scripture; it's the *travelers*.
Bragi with his harp, Lofn with her praise-poetry, Idunn with her
apples, Dagny with her new-day. Skalds who go from hall to hall
and sing what the town needs to hear but won't say itself.

When you build a Norse-flavored world, the player is walking through
a place that **knows itself through song** - not through law, not
through the church, not through the parish ledger. The bell rings
because someone sang a song about it. The keeper of the bookshop
remembers a line she heard from a traveler. The warden's sword is
named, because naming is what the skalds do.

A town in this flavor feels like:

- **A roadside with people coming and going.** Travelers, not
  residents, are the connective tissue. Most of the named figures
  arrived from somewhere and are leaving for somewhere else.

- **Verse in place of record.** Names get remembered; deeds get
  sung. The "law" is what the skalds agree on, and the skalds
  agree on what's beautiful, not what's accurate. Two travelers
  can sing the same battle differently and both be right.

- **Long things and short things in tension.** The skald sings
  twenty verses about one thing that took one breath. The
  wanderer says the whole thing in one sentence and is gone.
  Both are true.

- **Names as living things.** Names have weight; to name something
  is to be responsible for it. The wrong name is not "wrong" -
  it's a different relationship. The right name is what changes.

The model should produce textures and names that feel like *someone
said this on the road*. Not library prose. Roadside prose.
Warm, brief, a little crooked.
