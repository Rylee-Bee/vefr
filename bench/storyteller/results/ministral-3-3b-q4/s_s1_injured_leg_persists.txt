Your sword arcs toward the guard’s leg, but the moment the blade moves, he’s already stepped to the side. The gatehouse shadows stretch long and sharp, catching the light off his polished boots. The weight shifts under his boots, and your sword passes harmlessly into the earth. He’s not waiting—he’s watching, always calculating.

---

The edge of your blade touches skin. A hiss of breath, a flash of white cloth—his leg stiffens for a heartbeat, then resets, unflinching. The wound is shallow, but the guard’s eyes don’t look away. The gatehouse hums with what he’s still deciding.

---

His boots don’t carry you fast enough—each step is slower, heavier, like a man who has long since accepted his body isn’t his own anymore. The guard watches you from the edge of the yard, then turns, his wounded leg a deliberate half-step behind him, as if to remind you the gatehouse has its own weight to bear too.

---

He does not blink. The right leg still won’t carry him like it used to, but he holds his ground now. The guard’s eyes scan you—curious, calculating—as if deciding whether to trust a man who made no attempt to fight. The gatehouse looms behind him, silent.
