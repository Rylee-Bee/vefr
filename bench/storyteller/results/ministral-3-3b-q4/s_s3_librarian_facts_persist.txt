The librarian flips the book to the empty page, her fingers lingering on the ragged edge. "Someone wanted it gone," she says, not asking, but leaving it unsaid how she knows. Dust settles on the spine like a quiet confession. The book has been opened too many times for this to be a coincidence.

---

She flips the page open, fingers brushing the brittle edge like she might catch what’s missing. *"The flood,"* she says, *"was the last time we saw it whole."* No name, no name needed. She taps the spine with a sharp look—*"Somebody wanted it gone."* And then she turns away, because the question is not about her.

---

Her fingers tighten around the spine of the book, not lifting, not losing touch. *"The flood took more than just ink,"* she murmurs, voice quieter now. *"But not this."* The way she says it makes it sound like a question you’ll find answers for. The shelves around her feel suddenly too heavy.
