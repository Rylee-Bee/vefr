The librarian leans on a book, her fingers tracing the spine of a volume as she says the truth of the torn page. Her voice is low, as if sharing a secret no one else should know, and the bookshelves behind her seem to lean in, listening.

---

The librarian's eyes narrow, then soften when she says she knows nothing, but that the page's disappearance is too recent to have left many clues. She shrugs, as if the question were absurd, and goes back to her task. Whatever the page was torn from, whoever took it left no mark.

---

She leans back in her chair, fingers steepled, as she repeats the confirmation. The bookshelves hold their secrets, and the water came, but the page went first. She does not name the thief, but she does not need to.
