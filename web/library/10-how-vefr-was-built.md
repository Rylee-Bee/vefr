VEFR is a game studio you can walk around in. Every room has a job, and
every job has a helper who lives there. Nobody wrote it all at once. It
was built one small piece at a time, and every piece was checked before
it was kept.

* * *

**People decide. Helpers help.** The helpers in VEFR are small programs.
Some follow rules. Some are small AI models that read a template, a kind
of fill-in form, and suggest something. You always choose: keep it,
change it, or ask again. Nothing is kept behind your back.

* * *

**Small on purpose.** A big AI model needs a big computer, or money, or
the internet. VEFR tries the smallest helper that can do each job, so it
runs on an ordinary laptop, for free. To find the smallest one, the
studio holds contests called *cups*: every helper tries the same tasks,
and the checks mark them fair and square.

* * *

**Checks are friends.** A check is a little test that says yes or no:
does the map have walls all the way round? Can you reach every door? Did
the helper use only the words it was given? When a check fails, that's
not bad news. It's a clue. Fixing things with clues is most of what
programmers do all day.

* * *

**Honest scores.** Sometimes a helper looked brilliant and was cheating
by accident, like handing back an empty page that happened to pass. So
the checks got better. The rule in this studio: never say something
worked unless it really did.
