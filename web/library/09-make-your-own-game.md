You can make a game. It is smaller than it looks, and you already have the
Studio.

Every room here is a department, and each one has a single job. Walk them
in order and you end up with something you can play and hand to someone
else.

What you need before you start:

- one idea you can say in two sentences;
- one person it is for (someone specific, not everyone);
- the willingness to play it badly first.

* * *

## 1. The Desk

**Desk** is where a world begins. It holds the world you are making and
waits for its first sentence.

Say what the game is in two sentences. Say what the player does with their
hands. Then name the one verb they repeat most: talk, walk, cook, climb,
carry, fight.

The **Desk** keeps your words. Everything after this is built on them.

* * *

## 2. The Folks

**Folks** is the people of your world. Give one of them a want and a fear.

A person who wants something the player can give or take is worth ten who
only answer questions. Name them plainly. Let them be a little wrong about
themselves.

Write one line they would say when they are busy, and one when they are
not.

* * *

## 3. The Map Room

**Map Room** draws the world as it is found: ground, walls, doors, water.

Start with one room, and the place the player steps in. Add the next room
only when the first one bores you. Every square holds one kind of ground;
that is all a map is underneath.

This part is *level design*, and a single square is a *tile*. Small and
clear beats big and vague.

* * *

## 4. The Library

**Library** keeps the words: the Studio's own books, and the books your
world hands out.

Write one page of history the player can find. Hide it somewhere they have
to look. A world that tells you where it came from, a page at a time, feels
much bigger than it is.

* * *

## 5. The Casting Table

**Casting Table** is where a hard question gets a straight answer: cast a
rune, then read it honestly.

Not every game needs it. But when you are stuck between two good choices,
ask, and then decide anyway. The stones do not flatter.

* * *

## 6. The Floor

**Floor** is the game itself. Walk it. Bump into what is there. Find what
is broken.

Play it badly on purpose. Try to leave the map. Try to skip the first thing
you built. Every time you are bored or lost, you have found your next piece
of work.

* * *

## 7. The Chronicle

**Chronicle** writes what happened: each visit, each change, in order.

Read it before you start, so you know where you left off. It is also
*version control* in plain clothes: a moment you can cut back to when an
idea goes wrong.

* * *

## 8. The Hall

**Hall** is the wall of what you kept: the moments, the marks, the games
that made it out.

When something works, keep it here. A game you can point at is a game you
will finish.

* * *

## 9. The Archives

**Archives** holds the truth beneath: the notes behind each choice.

When two of your own decisions disagree, this is where you find out which
one is real. Keep the small ones; they are the ones you will need.

* * *

## 10. The Boiler Room

**Boiler Room** is the works behind the rooms: how the Studio reads, moves,
and sounds.

Set it up for the day you are having: bigger text, less motion, a quieter
screen. The game should fit the person playing it, and that starts with the
person making it.

* * *

Then show it.

When a game plays from the start to somewhere, it is real enough to share.
Send the file, or put it in the **Hall**, and watch someone else find their
way through it.

You made a game. Make the next one smaller, or stranger.
