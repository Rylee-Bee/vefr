Why keep going down a dark stair? Part of the answer is that you come
back up different.

A game can grow its hero in two ways. It picks one.

* * *

The first is levels. Every monster is worth some experience. Collect
enough and you reach the next level, and the hero gains a little
health and a little attack. The game tells you in plain words:
"You are stronger. Level 2."

The numbers set the pace. A level that comes quickly feels generous.
A level that comes slowly feels earned. Designers tune these by
playing, again and again.

* * *

The second is learning by doing. There are no levels and no bar. The
hero simply gets better at what she does. Strike a lot, and her
attack grows. Take a lot of hits, and her health grows.

You never watch a counter tick. One quiet line says it happened:
"All that striking is paying off."

Each skill has a cap, so it grows and then settles.

* * *

Both ways follow one rule. Growth only adds. You never lose a level
and a skill never fades. If a hero faints, she wakes up and everything
she learned is still hers.

That is what keeps a game cozy while it still rewards you. You can
take your time, and nothing you earn is taken away.

* * *

A game that wants neither just leaves growth out, and the hero stays
as she is. Nothing changes for games that never asked for it.
