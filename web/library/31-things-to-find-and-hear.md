Some of what makes a world feel lived in is not the story at all. It is
the small things you can wear, collect, hear and watch.

* * *

Gear. An item can name a slot: hand, body, head, feet or charm. Open the
bag and you will find a section for the hero herself, with one row for
each slot. Wear a thing and the numbers change; take it off and they
change back. Wearing something in a full slot hands the old thing back
to the bag. A key cannot be worn, so it can never go missing from its
door. Every change gets one plain sentence, shared by the eye and the
screen reader.

* * *

The album. A world can keep a shelf of stickers that fill in as you
play: met your first rat, entered the third floor, read a letter. Each
sticker is earned by something the story already knows how to notice.
Some show their name from the start. Some show only a riddle until you
find them. Some are secrets that only appear as a count. Open the album
from the menu. Stickers are only ever added, never taken away, and there
is no clock.

* * *

Sound. A world can ask for a few soft notes made right in the browser,
with no sound files to carry. There is one for a strike, a hit
taken, a fallen monster, a pickup, a door, a locked door, a new level, a sticker
and an ending. They are never music and never on a timer. Each one is
also said in words, so the game is fully playable on mute, and the
Display page in the menu has a switch to turn them off.

* * *

Walking. By default every character is one picture that hops a little as
it steps. A character can instead bring a sheet of frames: standing, and
a few steps in each direction. The game shows a frame as the character
walks and goes back to standing when the step ends. If a side is missing
the other side is drawn flipped, and if the sheet is missing the hop
stays. People who ask for less motion get the quiet version either way.

* * *

None of these is required. A world with no gear, no album, no sounds and
no sheets plays exactly as it always did. Add one at a time, and play
after each.
