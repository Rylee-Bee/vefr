Imagine a picture covered in snow, so thick you can't see anything. A
painting model learns one trick: look at the snowy picture and guess
which flakes to brush away.

* * *

It practises on thousands of real pictures. Each time, some snow is
added on purpose, and the model guesses what was underneath. At first
its guesses are terrible. After thousands of tries, they get good.

To paint something new, it starts from pure snow and brushes a little
away, again and again, until a picture appears. That's called
*diffusion*.

* * *

**Tags tell it what to paint.** Every practice picture comes with a few
words, like *tile, wall, stone*. Later, if you ask for *tree*, it brushes
the snow toward a tree.

**Small can be smart.** VEFR's own little painter is tiny compared with
famous models, because game tiles are tiny too. Its first pictures were
pure snow. Watching it learn, step by step, is part of the fun.

* * *

**Only learn from art you're allowed to.** Artists own their work. VEFR's
painter only learns from art that was given away freely (like Kenney's
packs, which anyone may use) or art made for this studio. Learning from
someone's work without permission isn't fair, even for a computer.
