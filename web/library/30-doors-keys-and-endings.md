A door that always opens is just a hole in a wall. A door that sometimes
says "not yet" gives the player something to want.

* * *

Any door or stair can ask for one thing: an item in the bag, or a flag
the story has set. When the hero tries it without, the game says one
plain sentence of your choosing, such as "The stair is shut. The great
pale moth wears the key on a bell." The hero stays where she is. With
the item in the bag, the door opens as it always did.

A key is kept, not used up. It stays in the bag, so the same door opens
every time.

* * *

Where does the key come from? Often from a creature. Give a tougher
monster the key to carry and it becomes a guardian: a small gear check
and a lock rolled into one. In Cottage of the Breeze the guardians sleep
until the hero bumps into them, so finding the right room is part of the
fun.

Two rules keep this cozy. A key never has a price, because a key sold to
the Trader could never come back. And a key must always be findable
before its own door.

* * *

You do not have to keep all of that in your head. Ask the game to check
the pack and it follows every lock from the start: which doors can be
opened with what the hero can reach so far, then which doors that
opens, and so on. If a key cannot be found, if it lies behind its own
door, or if it could be sold, it says so in a sentence that names the
key and the door.

* * *

Stories need an end, even when they keep going. One rule can finish a
story beat. When it fires, a small card appears with two choices: keep
exploring, or start over. Pressing the one button closes it and the
walk continues. It shows once per save, so a reload never repeats it,
and every word on it is real text for anyone using a screen reader.

The card marks that a part of the story is complete. What comes after is
up to the game: a way home, a treasure, a new set of stairs.

* * *

Try it in your own world. Put a door in the way, give a creature the
key, and write the sentence the hero hears when she is turned away.
Then write the last line you want her to read at the end, and let the
card close the beat.
